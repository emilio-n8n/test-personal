## 3. What I would fix first, and why

**`the_inertia_tensor_agrees_with_the_centroid_the_kernel_reports` first.** It is the only failure whose
"fix" could corrupt the kernel silently: a well-meaning fixer, seeing "Ixx is 32 but the parallel-axis theorem
says 158", is invited to *add an inertia tensor to the kernel that satisfies the test's wrong identity*, and
then the wrong physics lives in `crates/solid` forever. It is also the cheapest correct fix and the least
ambiguous: no kernel change, no design decision — the test computes J, then asserts Steiner for I. Repair the
identity in the test (either `predicted = add_outer(at_c, V · c⊗c)` for second moments, or convert
`I = tr(J)·δ − J` before `shift`), pin the closed form as diag(V·a²/12, …), re-run — and, in the same pass, the
fixed test keeps its stated purpose (a wrong kernel centroid does break the identity, I verified which quantity
is load-bearing).

Second would be the thin-feature `assert_eq!(resolved, 0)` — same class, but medium-high rather than
high confidence, because I could not exclude that a loop assertion also trips (a real defect).
